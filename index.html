01Abstract

Enforcing nonlinear contact constraints in receding-horizon model predictive control (MPC) for humanoid locomotion remains an open challenge. Reduced-order linear formulations sacrifice fidelity; nonlinear solvers struggle to respect friction cones, unilateral contact, and zero-moment-point bounds at real-time rates.

We present a hierarchical controller that combines constrained iLQR-MPC with whole-body control (WBC). The MPC operates over centroidal dynamics with footstep location as a state, co-optimising contact forces and footholds within a single receding-horizon problem. Swing-foot equality constraints are enforced via augmented Lagrangian penalties, while inequality constraints are enforced via relaxed logarithmic barriers. A whole-body controller then reconciles the MPC plan with full-body dynamics and maps the result to joint torques, preserving contact feasibility throughout.

Two layers, one plan: a reduced-model planner co-optimises contact forces and footholds over a receding horizon, and a whole-body controller realises that plan on the full 35-DoF multibody model.

In MuJoCo simulations on the Unitree G1 humanoid, the framework achieves stable locomotion and recovers from sagittal pushes of up to 245 N and lateral pushes of up to 175 N by jointly adapting contact forces and footstep placement online.

02Results

All clips are recorded in MuJoCo on the Unitree G1. Impulsive disturbances are applied to the torso as a constant force of 0.12 s duration at t = 15.0 s during forward walking at 0.1 m/s. Use the speed control under each clip to slow the recovery down.

2.1 Steady-state locomotion

Unperturbed forward walking at 0.1 m/s for 40 s (≈100 steps).

2.2 Push recovery

The push magnitude is increased incrementally until failure, defined as any non-foot body link contacting the ground. In every direction the recovery is produced by the same mechanism: the iLQR-MPC re-optimises footstep placement and contact-force distribution jointly over the receding horizon, and the WBC refines the updated plan into joint torques. Sagittal and lateral clips show the maximum recoverable force in each direction; oblique and force–torque clips are illustrative examples only, not limit cases.

A pronounced asymmetry: pushes directed toward the stance leg (−y, 175 N) are tolerated more than twice as well as pushes directed away from it (+y, 80 N). With the left leg in stance, a right-to-left push drives the CoM away from the support foot, shrinking the effective support polygon and leaving footstep adjustment as the sole recovery mechanism.
