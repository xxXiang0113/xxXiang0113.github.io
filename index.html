01Abstract

Humanoid locomotion control answers two questions at once: where to step, and how to distribute the contact force. A disturbance invalidates both the footstep and the contact force of the nominal plan, so recovery means revising them under constraints that switch with every footfall.

This paper treats them as a single receding-horizon problem: an iLQR-MPC over single-rigid-body (SRB) dynamics, which retains attitude dynamics and the full contact wrench, jointly optimizes contact forces and footstep locations, with contact and motion constraints handled in the backward pass through augmented-Lagrangian and relaxed-barrier terms. Foot placement is therefore re-optimized at every replan instead of delegated to a separate step planner. A whole-body controller then maps the plan to joint torques while preserving contact feasibility.

Because the algorithm is model-based, its behavior is interpretable: every command follows from an explicit cost and constraint set, so a push's effect traces to the constraints and to the re-optimized footstep, with no per-platform training.

In MuJoCo simulations, the iLQR-MPC replans at 100 Hz (mean solve time 8.9 ms); the Unitree G1 sustains steady-state walking and recovers from impulsive 245 N forward and 175 N lateral pushes.

2.2 Push recovery

The push magnitude is increased incrementally until failure, defined as any non-foot link contacting the ground. In every direction the recovery is produced by the same mechanism: the iLQR-MPC re-optimizes footstep placement and contact-force distribution jointly over the receding horizon, and the WBC refines the updated plan into joint torques. Sagittal and lateral clips show the maximum recoverable force in each direction; oblique and force–torque clips are illustrative examples only, not limit cases.

A pronounced asymmetry: left-to-right (−y) pushes are tolerated up to 175 N, whereas right-to-left (+y) pushes fail above 80 N, more than a twofold difference. With the left leg in stance, a left-to-right push is directed toward the stance leg, where the contact wrench redistributes to counteract it directly. Conversely, a right-to-left push drives the CoM away from the stance foot, shrinking the support polygon and leaving footstep adjustment as the sole recovery mechanism.

03Computational performance

Measured over approximately 9,000 MPC solves (27,000 AL/ReB outer iterations) during the disturbance-rejection experiments, on an Intel Core i5-12400F.

Across the disturbance-rejection experiments, the iLQR solve averages 8.26 ms; reference preparation and result conversion add 0.67 ms, for 8.93 ms end to end. Across push directions the iLQR solve mean varied between 8.19 and 8.32 ms, and individual solves reached 13.56 ms during push transients. The MPC is replanned every 10 ms with zero-order hold while the WBC runs at 500 Hz; during push transients, MPC solve times occasionally exceed the control period.
